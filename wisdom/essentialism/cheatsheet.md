# Cheatsheet — Essentialism (Greg McKeown)

## Decision rules
- New opportunity → "Is this the very most important thing I should be doing with my time right now?" Anything short of a definitive yes is a no.
- Selection with one dominant criterion → score 0–100; **< 90 = 0, reject**. "If it isn't a clear yes, it's a clear no" (Sivers/Vitsoe).
- Any screen → 3 minimum + 3 extreme criteria; fail any minimum OR < 2/3 extremes → no.
- Existing commitment → "If I weren't already invested, how much would I invest now?" If less than current, uncommit.
- Owned thing/role → "If I didn't own this, what would I pay to acquire it?"
- Two attractive options → don't ask "how do both?"; ask **"Which problem do I want?"** / "What do I want to go big on?"
- Every time estimate → **+50%** (1h call → 90min block). Project budgets → ~+20%.
- New request tempo → **slow yes, quick no**.
- Volunteering impulse → pause 5 seconds, ask "is this essential?" before speaking.
- Stalled system → fix the **one** constraint ("slowest hiker"); ask "which obstacle, if removed, makes most others disappear?" One at a time.
- Starting something big → smallest useful step at the earliest moment (10 min, two weeks early).
- In-play overwhelm → **W.I.N. "What's important now?"** If unclear, answering that IS the task.
- Stressed and cognitively degraded → prescribe play, not more effort. Tired → sleep is protecting the asset, not weakness.
- Crossroads in life → "What is essential?" Eliminate everything else.

## Saying no — pick a script by direction
| Situation | Script |
|---|---|
| Boss/upward | "Yes — what should I deprioritize?" |
| Peer/sideways | "You're welcome to X; I'm willing to Y" |
| Can't help | "I can't, but X might" |
| Need time | "Let me check my calendar and get back to you" |
| Deep-work block | Email bounceback ("In monk mode until …") |
| Soft landing | Awkward pause / "no, but" / humor |

Principles: separate the decision from the relationship; you're trading popularity for respect; a clear "I'm going to pass" beats vague stringing-along.

## Thresholds & numbers the author commits to
- 90 = the rejection line (90 Percent Rule).
- +50% on all time estimates; planning fallacy data: estimated 27.4 days, actual 55.5.
- Top performers: 20% effort → 80% results; up to 10,000× (Myhrvold).
- Sleep: 8h; best violinists 8.6h/24 + naps; one all-nighter ≈ 0.1% blood alcohol.
- Boundaries: **zero exceptions** — one breach collapses the wall.
- One priority per person at a time ("priorities" pluralized only in the 1900s).

## Tells & smells (you're sliding into Nonessentialism)
- Language: "I have to", "it's all important", "how can I fit it all in".
- A portfolio whose only common criterion is "a customer asked".
- Cutting 18 "priorities" to 17 — refusing the real exercise.
- "Too busy to attend the thinking day" → inefficiency or understaffing, by definition.
- Effort rising while results fall → get a neutral second opinion (no sunk costs).
- A felt "pinch" of resentment → a boundary just got crossed; write it down.
- Success arriving → paradox of success begins; re-clarify before the "go-to person" diffusion sets in.
