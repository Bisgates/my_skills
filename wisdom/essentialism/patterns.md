# Patterns — Essentialism (Greg McKeown)

## Minimum + Extreme Criteria Screen (Ch 9)
**When to use**: any incoming opportunity.
**How**: write the opportunity down; list 3 minimum criteria and 3 extreme (ideal) criteria. Fails any minimum OR fewer than 2 of 3 extremes → no.
**Trade-offs**: rejects many "good" options by design; that is the point.

## The 90 Percent Rule (Ch 9)
**When to use**: any selection with one dominant criterion.
**How**: score 0–100 on that criterion alone; anything below 90 becomes a 0. "How would a 65 on a test feel?"
**Trade-offs**: deliberately keeps you "understaffed" rather than wrongly committed (Vitsoe).

## Graceful-No Repertoire (Ch 11)
**When to use**: declining without damaging the relationship.
**How**: pick by situation — (1) awkward pause; (2) soft "no, but"; (3) "let me check my calendar and get back to you"; (4) email bounceback; (5) "Yes — what should I deprioritize?" (upward); (6) humor; (7) "You're welcome to X, I'm willing to Y" (sideways); (8) "I can't, but X might".
**Trade-offs**: short-term annoyance, long-term respect.

## Reverse Pilot (Ch 12)
**When to use**: recurring deliverable of suspected low value.
**How**: stop it silently for a few weeks; if nobody notices, eliminate permanently.
**Trade-offs**: brief invisible risk vs zero-confrontation elimination.

## Zero-Base Questions (Ch 12)
**When to use**: stuck in sunk cost or endowment.
**How**: "If I weren't already invested, how much would I invest now?" / "If I didn't own this, how much would I pay to buy it?" / "If I wasn't on this project, how hard would I work to get on it?"
**Trade-offs**: requires admitting past waste out loud.

## Neutral Second Opinion (Ch 12)
**When to use**: effort rising while results fall.
**How**: ask a sounding board with no sunk costs in the project.
**Trade-offs**: must confess frustration to use it.

## Add 50 Percent (Ch 15)
**When to use**: every time estimate.
**How**: 1-hour call → block 90 minutes; 10-minute drive → leave 15 early. ~+20% on project budgets.
**Trade-offs**: looks indulgent; counters the planning fallacy, which persists even in self-aware estimators.

## Five-Question Scenario Buffer (Ch 15, Michel-Kerjan)
**When to use**: planning anything with downside risk.
**How**: what risks? worst case? social effects? financial impact? how to invest to reduce risk? — the answer to Q5 is the buffer to build.
**Trade-offs**: extreme preparation costs now (Amundsen: 4 thermometers, 3 tons food vs Scott's 1/1/1).

## Constraint Diagnosis / Slowest Hiker (Ch 16)
**When to use**: stalled system or project.
**How**: (1) state the essential intent precisely ("How will we know we're done?"); (2) find the constraint — the stage with the biggest queue behind it, or "which obstacle, if removed, makes most others disappear?"; (3) remove it, one at a time; if it's a person, ask "what's blocking you, how can I help?"
**Trade-offs**: whole system temporarily paces at the constraint's speed.

## Minimal Viable Progress / Preparation (Ch 17)
**When to use**: starting anything big or fighting procrastination.
**How**: smallest useful step at the earliest moment — 10 minutes two weeks before deadline; 4 minutes to create the presentation file; probe cheaply before committing (tweet → article → book).
**Trade-offs**: feels trivially small; compounds instead of cramming.

## Cue Redesign (Ch 18)
**When to use**: a bad habit you keep losing to.
**How**: keep the existing cue, attach a new routine to it (journal placed next to the phone); change one routine at a time; hardest task first thing daily; themed days for recurring chaos (Dorsey: Mon mgmt, Tue product, …).
**Trade-offs**: early reps feel hard before the basal ganglia take over.

## Triage-the-Now (Ch 19)
**When to use**: many tasks vying at once, paralysis setting in.
**How**: stop → write down everything pulling at you → ask "what's important now?" (if unclear, answering that IS what's important now) → park future ideas on a separate "someday" list → work one item at a time, erase on completion.
**Trade-offs**: minutes of setup buys faster, better execution.

## Social Contract (Ch 14)
**When to use**: start of any collaboration.
**How**: state "here's what matters to me / here are my non-negotiables", ask the same back; set fences in advance so the direct "no" is never needed. Log every felt "pinch" of resentment to discover unstated boundaries.
**Trade-offs**: upfront candor; zero exceptions afterward — one breach collapses the wall (Christensen).

## Designed Thinking Space (Ch 5)
**When to use**: calendar full of other people's priorities.
**How**: graduated options — 5 min/day; 20 min of classic literature; 2h of blank calendar in 30-min blocks (Weiner); monthly no-call no-email day (O'Brien's Do-Not-Call Monday); twice-yearly Think Week (Gates).
**Trade-offs**: feels like indulgence before becoming the "single most valuable productivity tool".

## 90-Day Journal Review (Ch 6)
**When to use**: finding the signal in your own months.
**How**: write less than you feel like daily; every ~90 days spend an hour reading for patterns and headlines only, skipping details.
**Trade-offs**: details deliberately ignored.

## Visually Reward Progress (Ch 17)
**When to use**: behavior that otherwise needs constant policing.
**How**: make small wins visible (thermometer chart; token systems: 10 tokens/week, each = 30 min screen or $0.50, reading earns more).
**Trade-offs**: small setup cost; McKeown's result: screen time −90%, reading +90%.

## Essential Intent Drafting (Ch 10)
**When to use**: defining team/role/life purpose.
**How**: aim for inspirational AND concrete; test with "If we could be excellent at only one thing, what?" and "How will we know we're done?" — e.g. "everyone in the U.K. online by end of 2012". Stop wordsmithing, start deciding.
**Trade-offs**: eliminates a universe of options by design — that is its function.
