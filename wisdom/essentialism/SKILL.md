## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Less but better** (Dieter Rams, *Weniger, aber besser*) — the defining principle. The goal is the **highest point of contribution**: the right thing, the right way, at the right time. Not doing more things, not even doing fewer things — doing **only** the right things.

**The Essentialist method — Explore → Eliminate → Execute** (the closet metaphor): a repeating cycle applied to every commitment, not a one-time purge.
1. **Essence** (mindset, ch2–4): *Choose* ("I choose to", never "I have to" — choice is an action, not a thing), *Discern* (almost everything is noise: Pareto 80/20 up to power-law 10,000×; "you cannot overestimate the unimportance of practically everything"), *Trade-off* (every yes is several nos; ask "Which problem do I want?", never "How can I do both?").
2. **Explore** (ch5–9): create space to think before deciding — *Escape* (scheduled blank calendar), *Look* (find the lead, not the facts), *Play* (broadens options), *Sleep* (protect the asset), *Select* (extreme criteria).
3. **Eliminate** (ch10–14): *Clarify* (essential intent), *Dare* (graceful no), *Uncommit* (reverse pilot, zero-base questions), *Edit* (cut, condense, correct), *Limit* (boundaries with zero exceptions).
4. **Execute** (ch15–19): make doing effortless — *Buffer* (+50%), *Subtract* (remove the constraint), *Progress* (minimal viable progress), *Flow* (routine), *Focus* (W.I.N.).

**The 90 Percent Rule** (ch9): score an option 0–100 on the single most important criterion; below 90 → treat as 0 and reject. Companion screen: 3 minimum + 3 extreme criteria — fail any minimum or fewer than 2 of 3 extremes → no. "If it isn't a clear yes, it's a clear no."

**Essential intent** (ch10): one purpose that is both **inspirational AND concrete** ("everyone in the U.K. online by end of 2012") — one decision that settles a thousand later ones. Test: "If we could be excellent at only one thing, what?" and "How will we know we're done?" Unclear teams decay into politics or undirected "it's all good" drift.

**The graceful no** (ch11): separate the decision from the relationship; trade popularity for respect. Eight scripts, keyed by direction — upward: "Yes — what should I deprioritize?"; sideways: "You're welcome to X, I'm willing to Y". Clarity about your own essential intent is the force field that makes no easy.

**Uncommitting** (ch12): each bias has a named antidote — sunk cost → "If I weren't invested, how much would I invest now?"; endowment → "If I didn't own this, what would I pay for it?"; FOMO → **reverse pilot** (silently stop, see if anyone notices); status quo → zero-based budgeting; casual yes → 5-second pause.

**Buffer** (ch15): add **50%** to every time estimate; the planning fallacy survives self-awareness (estimated 27.4 days, actual 55.5). Prepare extremely because you cannot predict (Amundsen vs Scott).

**Subtract the constraint** (ch16): the system moves at the pace of its **slowest hiker**. State the intent precisely, find the one obstacle whose removal dissolves most others, remove it — one at a time. "Done is better than perfect."

**Paradox of success** (ch1, ch20): clarity → success → "go-to person" → diffused demands → lost clarity → undisciplined pursuit of more. The discipline is re-clarifying at every success. If you don't prioritize your life, someone else will.

**Protect the asset** (ch8): you are the instrument; an hour less sleep is not an hour more output (one all-nighter ≈ 0.1% blood alcohol). The type-A inversion: the hard, brave thing is the nap, not the all-nighter.
