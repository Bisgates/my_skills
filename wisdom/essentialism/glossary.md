# Glossary — Essentialism (Greg McKeown)

**boundary by design vs by default** — unset boundaries get set for you by others' demands (Ch 14)
**buffer** — built-in slack that prevents collisions between plan and surprise (Ch 15)
**casual commitment** — obligation acquired via offhand chat, never deliberately chosen (Ch 12)
**choice as action** — options are things, choice is an act; it can be forgotten but never taken away (Ch 2)
**chronos vs kairos** — quantitative clock time vs qualitative opportune time, which exists only in the present (Ch 19)
**clarity as force field** — internal clarity about the essential deflects nonessential requests; unclarity = defenselessness (Ch 11)
**condensing ratio** — words-to-ideas, effort-to-results, driven down by editing; not doing more at once, just less waste (Ch 13)
**constraint ("slowest hiker")** — the one obstacle holding the whole system back; improvements elsewhere are immaterial (Ch 16)
**cue–routine–reward** — Duhigg's habit loop; keep the cue, swap the routine attached to it (Ch 18)
**dealbreaker** — request type refused on principle unless it overlaps your priorities (Ch 14)
**decision fatigue** — the more choices forced, the worse their quality (Ch 1)
**decision = cutting** — Latin root *cis/cid*, "to cut/kill"; choosing means killing options (Ch 13)
**endowment effect** — overvaluing what you own (Kahneman's mugs: $5.25 ask vs $2.25 bid), why commitments are hard to drop (Ch 12)
**essential intent** — one decision both inspirational AND concrete that settles a thousand later decisions (Ch 10)
**extreme criteria** — deliberately over-strict thresholds that trade many "good" for one "perfect" (Ch 9)
**fear of waste** — Arkes's ski-trip study: most pick the worse $100 trip over the better $50 one (Ch 12)
**find the lead** — the essential meaning of a set of facts, not their summary (Ch 6)
**flow / play** — anything done simply for the joy of it; broadens options and sharpens executive function (Ch 7)
**graceful "no"** — declining the request while separating the decision from the relationship (Ch 11)
**highest point of contribution** — the right thing, the right way, at the right time (Ch 1)
**implicit criteria** — unspoken selection defaults like "the manager asked, so do it" (Ch 9)
**invisible art** — good editing is unseeable; subtraction that gives essentials space (Ch 13)
**kairos journal** — log opportune moments and their triggers, then re-create them (Ch 19)
**learned helplessness** — Seligman & Maier; shows up as checking out OR as hyperactive yes-saying (Ch 2)
**less but better** — Dieter Rams's *Weniger, aber besser*; the book's defining principle (Ch 1, Ch 3)
**majoring vs minoring** — Essentialism as core identity vs occasional practice; only the core compounds (Ch 20)
**metanoia** — an idea becoming emotionally true (heart, not just mind) so it changes behavior (Ch 20)
**minimal viable preparation** — the smallest possible investment at the earliest possible moment (Ch 17)
**minimal viable progress** — the smallest progress that is useful and valuable to the essential task (Ch 17)
**multifocus fallacy** — doing two things at once is possible; concentrating on two is not (Ch 19)
**normative conformity** — wired drive to meet group expectations that makes refusal physically uncomfortable (Ch 11)
**paradox of success** — clarity → success → "go-to" reputation → diffused effort → lost clarity (Ch 1, Ch 20)
**Pareto Principle / power law** — 20% of effort yields 80% of results; top performers can be 10,000× (Myhrvold) (Ch 3)
**the "pinch"** — twinge of resentment signaling a hidden boundary just got crossed (Ch 14)
**planning fallacy** — Kahneman 1979; thesis estimates 27.4 days best-case, actual 55.5; only 30% on time (Ch 15)
**poiesis** — Heidegger's "bringing-forth"; work as removal of obstruction (Ch 16)
**priority (singular)** — pluralized only in the 1900s; five priorities = none (Ch 1)
**progress principle** — Amabile & Kramer: progress in meaningful work is the top workday motivator (Ch 17)
**protect the asset** — you are the instrument of contribution; sleep and recovery are investments, not costs (Ch 8)
**regret asymmetry** — a no costs minutes of regret; an unwanted yes costs days to years (Ch 11)
**reverse pilot** — silently remove an activity and watch whether anyone notices (Ch 12)
**routine as default** — enshrine essentials in routine so execution is near effortless (Ch 18)
**slow yes / quick no** — Tom Friel's default response speeds for new commitments (Ch 11)
**social contract** — up-front mutual statement of goals and non-negotiables at the start of a collaboration (Ch 14)
**status quo bias** — continuing a practice only because it has always been done (Ch 12)
**straddling** — Porter: running incompatible strategies simultaneously; guarantees marginal results (Ch 2, Ch 4)
**sunk-cost bias** — continuing a known loser because of unrecoverable past investment (Concorde, ~$1B) (Ch 12)
**trade-off** — Sowell: "there are no solutions, only trade-offs"; every yes is several nos (Ch 4)
**vital few vs trivial many** — Juran's Law of the Vital Few; most activities are trivial, a few are vital (Ch 1, Ch 3)
**W.I.N.** — Gelwix's in-play question: "What's Important Now?" (Ch 19)
**windfall discipline** — Norway banked oil revenue into a $720B buffer; Britain spent its £166B (Ch 15)
**zero-based budgeting** — every commitment must re-justify itself from zero today (Ch 12)
**the 90 Percent Rule** — score 0–100 on the single top criterion; below 90 → treat as 0 and reject (Ch 9)
